Module 3: Second-Order Response

Explore the mass-spring-damper system and see how its parameters affect oscillation, overshoot and stability.

System Model

m ẍ + b ẋ + kx = F(t)

m = mass, b = damping, k = spring stiffness, F = applied step force

1. System Parameters

Move the sliders to change the mass, damping, spring stiffness and force.

2. Predict, Then Check

Choose your parameter values, then predict how the mass will move.

Will the response oscillate and overshoot the final position?

Hint: Try changing only damping (b). More damping can reduce oscillation. Your answer is checked using the current slider values.

5. State-Space Representation

6. Phase Plane

The graph shows how displacement and velocity change together as the mass moves.

The path approaches the equilibrium point, where the mass stops moving (velocity = 0).

Point coordinates appear on the graph; hover over the path to see more values.

7. Eigenvalues & S-Plane

Eigenvalues tell us whether the system is stable and whether its response oscillates.

Poles on the left side indicate stability. A nonzero imaginary part indicates oscillation.
